Methods

Period search

Times are in days (any origin; for real data use light-time corrected times), frequencies in cycles per day.

Generalised Lomb–Scargle (gls) fits a sinusoid plus a floating mean at each trial frequency, with weights (Zechmeister and Kürster, 2009). The power is the relative χ² reduction, between 0 and 1. A double-peaked asteroid light curve gives its strongest GLS peak at twice the rotation frequency.

FALC (falc, falc_fit) follows Harris et al. (1989): at each trial frequency a Fourier series of order $n$ is fitted together with one zero point per group (night, station, band, …),

\[m_i = Z_{g(i)} + \sum_{k=1}^{n} \left[a_k \cos(2\pi k f t_i) + b_k \sin(2\pi k f t_i)\right],\]

and the χ² is recorded. Order 2 or more resolves the two different halves of a double-peaked light curve, so the best FALC frequency is the rotation frequency. The free zero points absorb calibration offsets between nights and bands.

Spectral window (spectral_window): $W(f) = |\sum_i e^{2\pi i f t_i}|^2 / N^2$. Periodogram peaks at frequencies where the window is large (one cycle per sidereal day, 1.0027 c/d, for ground-based data) are aliases.

False-alarm probability (permutation_fap): the data are shuffled, within groups if given, and the maximum of the periodogram is compared with the observed one. Shuffling single points ignores correlated errors, so for sparse survey photometry with nightly offsets the FAP is too optimistic; shuffle nightly means or pass the nights as groups.

Folded light curves

fold gives the rotational phase, fourier_fit a weighted Fourier series in the phase.

Split-halves test (split_halves): the folded light curve is cut into its first and second half, which are overlaid and binned (Harris et al., 2014). If the halves agree, the half period describes the data equally well; if they differ, the full period is needed. A symmetric double-peaked light curve also has similar halves, so the test cannot always decide between $P$ and $2P$.

Phase curves

reduced_magnitude: $m - 5\log_{10}(r\Delta)$. fit_phase_curve fits the linear phase curve

\[m_\mathrm{red} = H + \beta\,\alpha + \Delta_\mathrm{band},\]

with one offset per band relative to a reference band, weighted least squares and iterative σ clipping. This is appropriate for distant objects observed at small phase angles (α ≲ 2°). ps1_to_V converts Pan-STARRS1 r and g − r to Johnson V (Tonry et al., 2012).

Physical quantities

geometric_albedo and diameter use $D = 1329\,\mathrm{km}\,p_V^{-1/2}\,10^{-H_V/5}$ (Harris and Lagerros, 2002). ellipsoid_amplitude is the light-curve amplitude of a triaxial ellipsoid at aspect angle θ (geometric scattering, zero phase angle) (Binzel et al., 1989).

CCD images and aperture photometry

Basic routines for reducing one's own frames to light curves; images are matrices img[x, y] in FITS axis order with pixel centres at integer coordinates. readfits needs using FITSIO (package extension), everything else has no extra dependency.

  • Reduction: masterframe (pixel-wise median of bias, dark or flat frames), calibrate (bias, scaled dark, median-normalised flat).
  • Detection and positions: findstars finds local maxima of the 3×3-smoothed image above a robust background threshold (robust_stats: median and 1.4826·MAD with one 3σ clip); centroid refines them by iterated intensity-weighted centroids after subtracting a local ring median. It reports a status (:ok, :edge, :noflux, :runaway when the position moves more than maxshift from the start, :maxiter) and returns NaN coordinates on failure by default, so that a failed fit (e.g. a faint predicted position drifting onto a brighter neighbour) cannot be mistaken for a measurement; fwhm_estimate gives the median FWHM from second moments.
  • Photometry: aperture sums a circular aperture with fractional edge pixels and subtracts the robust sky of an annulus; the error includes source photon noise, sky scatter and the error of the sky level (Howell, 1989). An aperture of ≈ 0.7–1 FWHM maximises the S/N for faint targets; then measure comparison stars with the same aperture so that the aperture correction cancels in differential photometry. instrumental_magnitude converts flux to magnitude.
  • Registration: match_offset finds the translation between frames by voting on pairwise star differences; platematch solves an affine plate solution against catalogue tangent-plane coordinates by a grid search over rotation and flip at known scale, followed by a least-squares fit.

Not included: PSF fitting, image alignment by resampling, cosmic-ray rejection, WCS handling (use the header WCS or platematch).
